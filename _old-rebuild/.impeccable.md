## Design Context

### Users
French residents of every age, often on a phone, who need to get a public-service task done (papers, taxes, health, housing, work) and don't know which of the hundreds of official sites to open. Many are stressed or not tech-savvy.

### Brand Personality
Calm, clear, welcoming. Three words: "patient, precise, generous". Emotional goal: relief — "someone finally put it all in one place". Always honest: an unofficial, independent project, never pretending to be the State.

### Aesthetic Direction
Faithful layout rebuild of america.gov (National Design Studio): huge editorial serif headline, neutral grotesk body, lots of white, big 40px-radius cards, navy primary buttons, pill ask bar that sticks to the bottom, playful inline animated "buttons" inside a giant statement sentence, grey "coming soon" section with floating UI previews, oversized wordmark footer.
France adaptation: EB Garamond (French Renaissance heritage) for display, Hanken Grotesk for text; navy + a rare tricolor red accent. Light theme (daytime admin tasks, anxious users on phones) with a dark-mode token set.
Anti-references: anything that looks like an official gouv.fr / DSFR site (no Marianne, no bloc-marque, no DSFR blue #000091 as brand), purple gradients, cards-in-cards.

### Design Principles
1. One input first: the question box is always one tap away.
2. Honesty is visible: unofficial notice at the top and bottom; AI answers show "Mistral" and their sources.
3. Motion explains, never decorates; everything respects prefers-reduced-motion.
4. Every live data widget degrades gracefully to a useful static fallback.
5. Plain French, vous, no jargon.
